The Solar System

The solar system consists of the Sun and everything that orbits it, including eight
planets, their moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets. The Sun contains about
99.8% of the total mass of the solar system.

The eight planets, in order from the Sun, are: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter,
Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. The four inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are
called terrestrial planets because they have solid, rocky surfaces. The four outer
planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) are called gas giants or ice giants because
they are primarily composed of hydrogen, helium, and other gases or ices.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system, with a mass more than twice that of
all other planets combined. Saturn is famous for its prominent ring system, made mostly
of ice particles with a smaller amount of rocky debris.

Earth is the only known planet to support life, largely due to the presence of liquid
water, a stable atmosphere, and a suitable distance from the Sun known as the
"habitable zone."

Pluto was reclassified from a planet to a "dwarf planet" in 2006 by the International
Astronomical Union, a decision that remains debated among scientists and the public.
