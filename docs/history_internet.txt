A Brief History of the Internet

The origins of the internet trace back to ARPANET, a project funded by the United
States Department of Defense in 1969. ARPANET connected computers at four universities
and was designed to allow research institutions to share computing resources and
survive network failures.

In 1983, ARPANET adopted the TCP/IP protocol suite, which became the standard method
for computers to communicate across interconnected networks. This transition is often
considered the technical birth of the modern internet.

Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN. The Web
introduced the concept of hyperlinks and web pages, making the internet accessible to
non-technical users for the first time. The first website went live in 1991.

The 1990s saw rapid commercialization of the internet, with the rise of browsers like
Netscape Navigator and search engines like Yahoo and later Google, founded in 1998.

Broadband internet began replacing dial-up connections in the early 2000s, enabling
richer multimedia content, video streaming, and eventually the rise of social media
platforms and cloud computing in the following decades.
